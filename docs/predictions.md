# Predictions, written down in advance

Recorded **7 September 2026**, before any of the Kilpatrick work was built and
before any baseline was taken.

The point of writing these now is that both outcomes are easy to explain after
the fact. If word-level skill moves, that will look like proof the habit
reading was right. If it does not move, that will look like proof a processing
weakness was there all along. Neither conclusion is worth much unless the
thresholds were fixed before the data arrived — so here they are.

Nothing here decides anything on its own. These are inputs to a conversation
with the evaluator, not a diagnosis; software cannot make that call and this
file should not be read as trying to.

---

## The two readings

**A — habit.** Early shortcut-taking (recognise-and-guess rather than decode)
hardened into a real skill gap. The phonological machinery is intact and
disused. Prediction: once the intermediate step is forced and rewarded,
word-level skills move in weeks.

**B — processing weakness.** Some component — most plausibly automaticity of
advanced phoneme manipulation, or rapid naming — is weak, so words never map
to memory efficiently however often they are decoded. Prediction: gains come
only from directly training the weak component, and come slowly.

---

## Primary outcome

**Nonsense-word decoding**, measured weekly on fresh items, never reused.
Accuracy and median response time both recorded.

| Reading | Prediction at 8 weeks, given ≥3 sessions per week |
|---|---|
| **A** | accuracy up **≥15 percentage points** from baseline; median response time down **≥30%**; most taught patterns read as whole units rather than sounded out |
| **B** | accuracy up **<5 points**; response time roughly flat; still sounding out letter by letter on taught patterns |

Anything between 5 and 15 points is genuinely ambiguous and gets called that.

## Secondary outcomes

| Measure | A predicts | B predicts |
|---|---|---|
| Nonsense-word **spelling** | ≥20 point gain | little movement |
| Look-alike discrimination time | falls steadily from the first fortnight | stays slow |
| PAST levels (if any were non-automatic) | reach automatic within 3–4 weeks | move slowly or not at all |
| Oral-reading miscues | first-letter and context guesses fall sharply | error *mix* unchanged, only volume shifts |

## What would change my mind about the framing itself

- **PAST all automatic at baseline** — removes Kilpatrick's phonemic-automaticity
  explanation entirely, and the plan's Branch B core with it. Then flat
  nonsense-word decoding points at rapid naming or working memory, not phoneme
  awareness, and argues for a CTOPP-2.
- **Distorted text (Ransom Note) fails badly** — suggests letter-sound
  knowledge is weaker than assumed, which no amount of morphology will fix.
- **Fast gains that do not transfer** to real reading or spelling — would mean
  the app is training itself rather than reading, which is the failure mode
  worth watching for hardest.

## Recorded caveats

- Rapid automatized naming has never been measured. It is the commonest second
  deficit and would explain effortful-but-accurate decoding.
- The full evaluation report has not been seen, and whether the "not dyslexic"
  conclusion rested on timed or untimed phonological measures is unknown. It
  matters: an untimed composite cannot rule out slow manipulation.
- Compliance is a confound. A flat result with three sessions a week means
  something; a flat result with three sessions a fortnight means nothing.
  Session counts are logged and must be reported alongside any conclusion.
